description: >-
  <p>A two-semester, master’s-level team project for COM4525, completed by a
  six-person team in partnership with the University of Sheffield spinout
  epiGenesys. The goal was to develop a startup minimum viable product using
  Lean Startup practices.</p><p>I proposed the initial concept: a
  career-guidance platform that helps users explore possible career transitions
  based on transferable skills rather than job titles. Instead of presenting
  careers as a traditional ladder, the system visualised them as an
  interconnected tree, showing how users could move between different career
  branches from their current position.</p><p>I led much of the initial
  discovery and product research, including 30 to 35 customer interviews,
  persona development, user-experience and pain-point mapping, story cards, and
  the creation of a Lean Canvas business model. These findings shaped the final
  product brief and ensured that development was grounded in validated user
  needs.</p><p>On the engineering side, I worked across the Rails 7 stack and
  led the implementation of the JavaScript-based career graph. The matching
  engine used breadth-first search to calculate hop distances between careers
  and scored potential transitions against the SFIA skills framework. A Delayed
  Job background worker allowed the graph to be recalculated without blocking
  user requests.</p><p>I maintained oversight of the project’s most technically
  complex features, with particular responsibility for testing, performance,
  security, and accessibility. Security was implemented using Devise
  authentication and CanCanCan role-based access control, alongside Have I Been
  Pwned k-anonymity password checks. The application was also assessed using
  Brakeman and Bundler Audit static security scans.</p><p>Testing included RSpec
  unit tests, Capybara and Selenium end-to-end workflows, performance testing,
  and an internally developed analytics layer for monitoring lead capture and
  conversion without relying on third-party tracking. I designed the structure
  of the team’s testing documentation and directed its production, which
  received the project’s highest individual assessment area.</p><p>A dedicated
  user-experience testing phase included moderated think-aloud sessions, WCAG
  contrast checks, keyboard-only navigation, and simulated colour-vision
  constraints. This process identified genuine accessibility issues, including
  low-contrast controls and filter dropdowns that could not be accessed using
  the keyboard. These issues were corrected and then retested, with libraries
  selected specifically to support reliable tab navigation.</p><p>The project
  received a mark of 75% and was recognised particularly strongly for its
  testing documentation, security, performance engineering, and accessibility
  work.</p>
