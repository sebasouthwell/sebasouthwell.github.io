description: >
  A two-semester team project (COM4525) built for University of Sheffield
  spinout epiGenesys — the initial concept, a career-guidance platform that
  matches users to adjacent careers by skill overlap rather than job title,
  was one I proposed and helped shape into the team's brief. I ran the Lean
  Startup discovery work myself: 30-35 customer interviews, two personas,
  experience/pain-point mapping and a full Lean Canvas business model, before
  any code was written. On the engineering side I worked across the Rails 7
  stack, including the career-matching engine — a BFS-precomputed hop-distance
  graph over careers (see the diagram) scored against the SFIA skill
  framework, backed by a DelayedJob background worker so the graph recalculates
  without blocking requests. Security followed Devise + CanCanCan RBAC with
  HaveIBeenPwned k-anonymity password checks, and was verified with Brakeman
  and Bundler Audit static scans. Testing combined RSpec unit specs with
  Capybara/Selenium end-to-end flows, plus an in-house analytics layer (no
  third-party trackers) for lead capture and conversion. A dedicated UX
  testing round — moderated sessions, WCAG contrast checks, keyboard-only and
  simulated colour-blind passes — caught real accessibility gaps (low-contrast
  buttons, un-tabbable filter dropdowns) that were then fixed and re-tested.
  Marked highly for its testing, security and performance engineering (75%).
