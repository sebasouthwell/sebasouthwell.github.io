description: >-
  <p>Updated to include the Streamlit demonstrator and presentation
  work:</p><p>A two-semester, master’s-level group research project for COM4520,
  completed in collaboration with the government body Building Digital UK. The
  project investigated methods for reducing hallucinations in large language
  models and enabling government data to be used in a reliable, secure, and
  informative way for employees.</p><p>Due to unforeseen circumstances, the team
  was without an academic supervisor during the first semester. I therefore
  assumed a leading role within the group, helping to define the project scope,
  establish the research direction, and coordinate the investigation. I led the
  system architecture and focused my research on agentic approaches, retrieval
  techniques, and output-verification strategies.</p><p>We evaluated a wide
  range of variables, including embedding models, language models, retrieval
  methods, preprocessing strategies, and verification techniques. As part of
  this work, we devised a custom verification method inspired by knowledge
  graphs and logical axioms. The approach aimed to decompose source documents
  into objective statements that could be compared against generated answers,
  providing a structured method for identifying unsupported claims.</p><p>The
  final system consisted of separate preprocessing and query-time pipelines.
  During preprocessing, documents were uploaded to Amazon S3, divided into
  multimodal elements, and prepared for retrieval. Infrastructure was
  provisioned using Terraform and AWS, with secure, least-privilege access to
  Amazon Bedrock. Preprocessing reduced query latency and token consumption by
  completing computationally intensive work before user requests were
  received.</p><p>Content was embedded using two approaches. Local CLIP
  embeddings combined textual and visual information within ChromaDB, while
  Amazon Bedrock Titan generated cloud-based multimodal embeddings. Video and
  audio content could also be processed, transcribed, and incorporated into the
  retrieval system. In parallel, an LLM-based graph transformer extracted
  entities and relationships into a Neo4j knowledge graph.</p><p>At query time,
  the system combined vector similarity search with structured graph traversal.
  This hybrid retrieval approach allowed generated answers to be checked against
  explicit entity and relationship evidence rather than relying on semantic
  similarity alone. Verification was further supported through subject, action,
  and verb parsing to compare responses against information extracted from the
  knowledge graph.</p><p>The architecture was designed to remain
  provider-agnostic. A configurable model catalogue supported OpenAI models,
  Amazon Bedrock models such as Claude, Llama 3, and Mistral, as well as Gemini,
  xAI, and locally hosted Ollama models including DeepSeek R1, LLaVA, and
  Qwen2.5.</p><p>We assessed each configuration using BERTScore and ROUGE-L
  across 275 benchmark runs. The project also included a pytest test suite,
  GitHub Actions continuous integration and deployment, Discord build
  notifications, and secure credential management throughout.</p><p>To
  demonstrate the system, we developed an interactive Streamlit interface that
  allowed users to submit queries, explore retrieved evidence, and review
  generated responses. As a team, we also communicated our research through
  interim presentations and a final academic poster presentation, explaining the
  system architecture, experimental methodology, findings, and practical
  implications to both technical and non-technical audiences.</p><p>The project
  received a mark of 74%.</p>
